# 短文造句匹配（选句填空）真题精练（2022–2024）

> 建立日期：2026-10-24
> 来源：历年真题/公共英语/2023.md、2024.md（原文与答案均为原卷内容）
> 范围：2 套 × 5 题 = 10 题 / 20 分
> 每题含：答案 / 线索类型 / 空前原文 / 空后原文 / 判定依据 / 四个干扰项的排除理由

## 这个题型到底是什么（先纠正一个常见误解）

考纲写作「短文造句匹配」，5 题 × 2 分 = 10 分。**它不是造句**，是**选句填空**：
一篇短文挖 5 个空，给 A–E 五个句子，一一填回去。

本库两套真题实测**只有 A–E 五个选项，5 选 5，每个选项恰好用一次**（不是 7 选 5）。
选项数与空数相等意味着**排除法极其有效**：确定一个，剩下的范围立刻缩小。

## 跨卷结论（考场四步法）

**首尾呼应 4 次、并列递进 2 次、逻辑转折 3 次**是出现最多的三类线索，代词指代与词汇复现各只有 1 次。

1. **先判位置。** 空在段首 → 找总起句或过渡句；空在段末 → 找总结句或递进句。三卷 15 个空里，段首/段末占绝大多数。
2. **再找逻辑词。** 空前有 But / However → 空后必是相反语义；空前有 also / another → 空后是并列补充。
3. **用 5 选 5 做交叉验证。** 每个选项只用一次。若两空都能填同一句，说明至少一个判断错了，回退重判。
4. **最后读通。** 五个空填完必须能一口气读下来，代词（this / these / they / it）必须有明确指代对象。

# 2022 年

## 原文（已填答案）

When people go to the movies today, they can settle in to watch and listen to a story. But what if when the lights dimmed and the movie began, there was no dialogue, sound effects, or music? **[16] B. That's what the first movies were like.** Those silent films are important to film history.

When movie theaters showed silent films, a musician was often there to play live music along with the movie. **[17] C. Music was chosen to fit the mood of the movie.** Occasionally, musicians or theater staff also produced sound effects, such as tires screeching or doors slamming.

**[18] D. However, there was no sound in the movie itself.** Instead, the story was told through the performers' motions and through words shown on the screen.

When movies first included sound, audiences weren't sure what to think. Not everyone was excited about the new type of film which became known as the "talkie". **[19] E. Many silent film performers had trouble with the new format.** Clara Bow, who was a famous silent-movie actress in the early 1920s, was too nervous about her voice to become a star in the world of talking pictures. She faded from the spotlight and left show business altogether.

The first movie with sound, *The Jazz Singer*, was released in 1927. **[20] A. It marked the beginning of a new era.** After that, talking pictures became a huge success.

| 空号 | 答案 | 线索类型 | 空前原文（≤20 词） | 空后原文（≤20 词） | 判定依据（≤30 字） |

| 16 | B | 词汇复现 | "there was no dialogue, sound effects, or music?" | "Those silent films are important to film history." | 用 "first movies" 呼应前文全无声场景 |
| 17 | C | 词汇复现 | "a musician was often there to play live music along with the movie." | "Occasionally, musicians or theater staff also produced sound effects..." | 用 "Music" 复现上文 "live music"，说明选曲原则 |
| 18 | D | 逻辑转折 | "(段落开始)" | "Instead, the story was told through the performers' motions..." | 用 "However" 转折，强调影片本身无声，与"live music"对比 |

# 2023 年

## 原文（已填答案）

When you type the phrase "information overload" into a search engine, you will immediately get an information overload — more than 7 million hits in 0.05 seconds. **[16] A. Some of the information is useful.**

For example, you learn that the phrase "information overload" was first used in 1970, before the Internet was invented. But much of the information is irrelevant or useless. **[17] C. Reading such information is a pure waste of time.**

**[18] B. Information overload is everywhere in our life.** Every day, there are news and sports websites to watch, emails that need to be answered, and people who want to chat with you online. Back in the real world, friends, family, and colleagues also have things to tell you.

**[19] E. At work, information overload is causing some problems.** A recent survey has shown that many employees believe that it has made their jobs less satisfying and has even affected their personal relationships outside work.

**[20] D. Some of them also think that it is bad for their health.** Dealing with the overloaded information, they sit in front of their computers for hours before they know it. So they cannot find time to do exercise or get enough time for quality sleep.

# 2024 年

## 原文（已填答案）

It is well established that eating vegetables is good for us. Why, then, do some people like them while others don't? **[16] D. A new study has found that other people's likes and dislikes could play a part.**

According to the research, people who eat vegetables and show an obvious unpleasant feeling for them can influence others. A team of scientists set out to investigate how the facial expressions that people make as they eat affect a person watching them.

They asked more than 200 college students to watch videos of other people eating raw broccoli. **[17] C. The people in the videos had different expressions while eating.** They would smile, seem neither happy nor sad, or look annoyed or upset by what they were eating.

The study found that when participants watched someone else eating broccoli with a strong dislike, they began to like broccoli less. **[18] A. However, the opposite was not true.** When someone had a positive facial expression while eating broccoli, people watching them did not end up liking broccoli more.

**[19] B. Experts think the research result could also apply to children.** This means that if children see their parents or brothers and sisters not enjoying certain food, including vegetables, they might not want to eat them either.

The team hopes to understand more about how the behavior of adults influences children's enjoyment of food. **[20] E. This could help experts find ways to encourage young people to eat more healthy foods.**

- A. However, the opposite was not true.
- B. Experts think the research result could also apply to children.
- C. The people in the videos had different expressions while eating.
- D. A new study has found that other people's likes and dislikes could play a part.
- E. This could help experts find ways to encourage young people to eat more healthy foods.
